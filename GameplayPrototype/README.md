Gameplay Prototype: Music Drift Runner
======================================
Playable link:https://kirikomeow.github.io/CMPM120-Final-project/GameplayPrototype/index.html

Music Drift Runner is the core gameplay prototype for our final project. It is a rhythm-driven arcade runner made with Phaser. The player controls a neon car, dodges beat-spawned obstacles, and tries to build score and combo while the road speed increases over time.

How to Play
-----------

- Open `index.html` in the `GameplayPrototype` folder, or launch it through the project index page.
- Press `Touch to Play` or press `Space` on the menu.
- Move left and right with the arrow keys or `A` / `D`.
- Hold `Shift` while moving to drift.
- On touch screens, drag or hold on the road to steer the car.
- Avoid obstacles. Passing obstacles increases combo and score.
- Use `Fullscreen` on the title screen for mobile or presentation testing.

Core Gameplay Loop
------------------

The core loop is: start a run, react to the rhythm, dodge incoming obstacles, drift on beat for bonus points, survive as long as possible, then submit a score to the local leaderboard after crashing.

Requirement Coverage
--------------------

### Audio

The prototype uses multiple audio behaviors generated at runtime with the Web Audio API:

1. Dynamic background music and rhythm backing:
   - `BeatManager` creates a repeating 120 BPM music system.
   - Each beat triggers synthesized kick, hi-hat, bass, chord, and arpeggio layers.
   - The music is synchronized with obstacle spawning and visual beat pulses.

2. Event-triggered sound effects:
   - A crash sound plays when the player overlaps an obstacle.
   - The crash combines noise, pitch drop, and ringing tones so it is distinct from the background music.

These sounds are not static audio files. They are dynamically generated by code, which fits the assignment category for dynamically generated sound. The crash effect is also triggered by a game event, while the background music loops continuously during play.

### Visual

The prototype uses several visual systems beyond a plain scene background:

1. Procedurally defined imagery:
   - The scrolling road grid, neon borders, beat flash, spectrum bars, player car, and obstacles are drawn with Phaser display primitives and updated every frame.
   - Beat events change colors, alpha, scale, and road presentation.

2. Particle and additive visual effects:
   - The player car creates drift sparks with a Phaser particle emitter.
   - Obstacle glows, player glow, beat flashes, and screen effects use additive blend modes and tweens to create a rhythm-reactive neon style.

The visual design is intentionally tied to the music system: beats pulse the road, UI, player glow, obstacle presentation, and spectrum bars.

### Motion

Motion is central to the prototype:

- The player car moves through Phaser Arcade Physics.
- Obstacles scroll down the road at increasing speed.
- The road grid scrolls continuously to create forward motion.
- Drifting changes acceleration, drag, top speed, car rotation, and particle behavior.
- Tweens animate beat pulses, obstacle spawns, UI feedback, title screen motion, and the crash sequence.

### Progression

The prototype demonstrates progression across a single gameplay session:

- Score increases over time and when obstacles are passed.
- Combo increases when the player successfully survives obstacles.
- The best combo for the run is tracked.
- Road speed increases during play, making the game harder over time.
- A local leaderboard stores the top five scores in `localStorage` after each crash.

### Prefabs / Reusable Objects

The prototype uses reusable object and scene classes:

- `PlayerCar` subclasses `Phaser.GameObjects.Container` and manages player visuals, physics, drift state, glow, and particles.
- `Obstacle` subclasses `Phaser.GameObjects.Rectangle` and manages obstacle size, color, glow, physics body, scroll speed, and cleanup.
- `MenuScene`, `GameScene`, and `GameOverScene` subclass `Phaser.Scene` to separate title, gameplay, and results flow.

Implementation Notes
--------------------

- Main gameplay code: `gameplay.js`
- Phaser runtime: `lib/phaser.js`
- Entry page: `index.html`
- The prototype is self-contained and does not require a build step.
- Audio starts only after the player begins the game, which avoids browser autoplay restrictions.
